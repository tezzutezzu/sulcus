RING PRESS MOULD - PLA POSITIVE, PLASTER CAST, CLAY
===================================================

This guide describes the chosen process:

  1. print the ring positive in PLA;
  2. pour plaster around the positive;
  3. remove the positive and use the plaster mould for clay.

The site exports two files:

  - landscape.stl: the ring positive, the file to print in PLA;
  - mould.stl: the negative ready for direct TPU printing, an alternative
    route that is not used for plaster.

The principle is simple: the plaster copies the positive and takes its
negative. The cavity formed in the plaster is the same one the program
computes for mould.stl, obtained by casting instead of printing.


1. WHAT TO PRINT
----------------

In the Generate tab set:

  Relief          14 mm
  Base            3 mm
  Diameter        160 mm
  Inner diameter  40 mm
  Arc             330 deg

Then press Generate and download landscape.stl.

The "Clay mould" group (Mould relief, Slope limit, Mould scale, Back) only
affects mould.stl, the TPU negative. It is not used for the plaster route.

Print landscape.stl in PLA with the flat base on the build plate:

  - 0.12-0.2 mm layers (0.16 is fine);
  - 2-3 perimeters and 10-15% infill;
  - no supports;
  - the relief surface is what matters: use 0.12 mm if you can.

The positive is at most 17 mm tall (3 mm base + 14 mm relief).


2. PREPARE THE POSITIVE
-----------------------

Before pouring the plaster:

  - sand the layer lines, especially on the relief and the side walls
    (400-600 grit);
  - add a 2-3 degree draft to the outer, inner and both end walls. The
    walls are printed vertical: over 17 mm, 2-3 degrees is about
    0.6-0.9 mm. Sand them with a tilted block, or file them by hand
    against a reference;
  - seal the surface with a primer or a thin filler. PLA is already
    water-resistant, but a closed surface helps release;
  - apply a thin release agent: a soap solution or a coat of PVA. Avoid
    oils and silicone, which can clog the plaster pores or stain the clay.

Draft matters because plaster and PLA are both rigid. The 30 degree gap
helps move the part, but it does not replace draft.


3. POUR THE PLASTER
-------------------

Build a box with:

  - footprint: ring outside diameter + 25 mm per side
    (for 160 mm: about 210 x 210 mm);
  - height: tallest relief + 25 mm (about 42 mm).

Place the positive INSIDE the box with the flat base on the bottom and the
relief facing up. The bottom must be flat and sealed.

Use mould-making plaster (pottery plaster, hydrocal or equivalent), not
hardware-store wall plaster. Follow the manufacturer's ratio; typically
100 parts plaster to 70-80 parts water by weight.

  - sprinkle the plaster into the water, not the other way round;
  - let it slake for 1-2 minutes;
  - mix for 2-3 minutes, slowly, without whipping in air;
  - pour from one corner in a continuous stream;
  - tap the box gently to release bubbles.

Pour until the plaster covers the relief by 20-25 mm.

Setting takes about 20-40 minutes. Remove the box and the bottom after
45-60 minutes, while the plaster is still slightly warm.

Now pull the positive downwards: its flat base is against the bottom, the
plaster surrounds it, and the relief is buried in the plaster. Pull the
positive down without levering on the cavity edges. If it resists, tap the
box gently.

Turn the block over: the cavity now faces up.


4. DRYING AND USE
-----------------

Wet plaster does not absorb water and the clay will stick. Let it dry:

  - 2-7 days in air, or
  - 24 hours at about 50 degrees.

Once dry, the mould is ready. Press clay into the cavity, strike the excess
off level with the rim, and let the piece firm up before demoulding. The
porous plaster absorbs water and helps release; no release agent is needed
for the clay.

Do not soak the mould and do not wash it under the tap. Repair chips with
fresh plaster.


5. WHY IT WORKS
---------------

The ring is a heightfield solid:

  - the base is flat;
  - the top surface has a single height at every point;
  - the walls are vertical.

There are no undercuts, so the positive can leave the plaster by pulling it
downwards once it has draft. The plaster cavity is the negative of the
surface: where the ring is tall, the plaster has a deep pocket; where it is
flat, a shallow pocket.

For the TPU negative the program also caps the slope at 60 degrees, so the
grooves do not become narrow slots. The PLA positive uses the original
surface reduced to 14 mm: if a ridge is very steep, sand it by hand before
pouring the plaster.


6. DIFFERENCES FROM TPU
-----------------------

  - Plaster is porous, rigid, cheap and releases clay well.
  - Plaster is brittle: use 20-25 mm walls and, if you like, a fibreglass
    mesh or a rigid box as backing.
  - Plaster needs draft and drying; TPU does not, but it prints slowly and
    releases worse.
  - Weight: a 210 x 210 x 42 mm block weighs a few kilograms. Fine for a
    bench tool, not something to move often.


7. USEFUL NUMBERS
-----------------

  Outside diameter        160 mm
  Inside diameter         40 mm
  Arc                     330 degrees
  Base                    3 mm
  Relief                  14 mm (17 mm maximum with the base)
  Draft                   2-3 degrees
  Plaster behind relief   20-25 mm
  Plaster walls           20-25 mm
  Plaster/water           100 : 70-80 by weight
  Setting                 20-40 minutes
  Drying                  2-7 days, or 24 hours at 50 degrees
  Clay (solid)            about 335 g at 1.8 g/cm3

Clay shrinkage during drying and firing is about 5-10%; it is not
compensated in the mould. If you need the final size exactly, enlarge the
model or accept the shrinkage.


8. FILES AND COMMANDS
---------------------

  landscape.stl   positive to print in PLA (this route)
  mould.stl       negative for direct TPU printing (alternative route)
  npm run mould   writes the offline negative to generated/mould-tpu.stl

The explanation of how the negative is built still holds: the plaster cavity
is the same geometry, obtained by casting.
